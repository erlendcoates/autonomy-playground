State Observer

A ball and beam held by a PD controller, simulated as in the video: the true ball runs the nonlinear model (g sin θ, the centrifugal term, the servo's lag and rate limit and its linkage) at 1 kHz, and the distance sensor measures p every Δt = 20 ms with noise σ. Three velocity estimates run side by side on the same measurements: the finite difference, the filtered derivative and the Luenberger observer on the linear model. Choose which one feeds the controller, drag the ▲ under the beam to move the desired position r, and press start the observer in the wrong place to watch it recover.

Offsets the observer's state by p̂ + 8 cm, v̂ − 0.10 m/s, as in the video's “choosing the gains”.
x̂k+1 = Ad x̂k + Bd θk + L (yk − C x̂k)
p̂k+1 = p̂k + Δt v̂k + l1 (yk − p̂k)
v̂k+1 = v̂k + Δt b θk + l2 (yk − p̂k)
ek+1 = (Ad − LC) ek,  Ad − LC = [1 − l1, Δt; −l2, 1]
b = 5/7 g = m/s² per rad, Δt = 0.02 s

The lag is the delay (in whole samples) that best lines the estimate up with the true velocity; the finite difference and the filtered derivative use the measurement as their position, so their position error is the sensor's.

Things to try

  • Feed the PD with the finite difference, then the filtered derivative, then the observer, and watch the beam and the beam jitter under the table: the finite difference makes the beam shake, the filtered derivative still makes it jitter, and with the observer the motion is smooth.
  • Slide the cut-off ωc from 5 to 40 rad/s and read the table: a high cut-off follows the true velocity with little lag but lets the noise through; a low one is smooth but lags (about 1/ωc). Then double σ: the finite difference's error doubles with it (√2 σ/Δt).
  • Press start the observer in the wrong place with the video's L, then with 3 l1 and l1/3, then 3 l2 and l2/3. A larger l1 snaps p̂ onto the noisy measurements; a smaller one makes both estimates oscillate. A larger l2 overshoots and is noisier; a smaller one is smooth but slow to recover.
  • Feed the PD with the filtered derivative, tick observer open loop and start it in the wrong place: the simulator's error never dies out, it grows, because the ball and beam is unstable on its own (both eigenvalues of Ad sit on the circle, at 1). Untick it and the feedback pulls the estimate back.
  • Drag l2 up past l1/Δt (or l1 towards 2.5): when an eigenvalue of Ad − LC leaves the unit circle the estimate blows up, and a PD fed by it loses the ball.
