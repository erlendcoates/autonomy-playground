MPPI

Model predictive path integral control (MPPI) steers an Otter uncrewed surface vehicle (USV) through Nørvasundet in Ålesund. At every step it adds random wiggles to its current plan, simulates every plan, weights each by its cost, and carries out the first step of the weighted average. Drag the square handles to reshape the path, or switch to collecting drifting litter.

Things to try

  • Set steer by to the cheapest plan. Each new set of random plans has a different winner, and each winner keeps all of its random wiggle: the amber plan on the map changes from step to step, and the planned turn rate jumps. Back on the weighted average, the errors partly cancel. Some jitter is left, and it is visible in the turn-rate chart.
  • Change the temperature T and watch the vote chart. Low: only a few cheap plans count, the effective number of plans falls towards one, and the jitter grows. High: all plans count about equally and the Otter responds late, or not at all.
  • Take M down to 10 and up to 5000. The time per replan grows in proportion, but the number of plans does not depend on the horizon: try 60 s. Every plan is simulated on its own, which is why MPPI suits a GPU.
  • Set σ to almost nothing: the plans stay close to the current one, and the Otter cannot find its way round a corner in time. Set α to 0: the wiggles are white noise, they cancel within a plan, and the plans hardly spread.
  • Drag a corner of the path onto land. Plans into shallow water are dropped, so the Otter cuts inside the corner and rejoins the path as soon as it can.
  • Drop a buoy right ahead with Buoy ahead, or pause and drag it. The plans that reach its circle turn red and get no vote, and the Otter passes just outside the 3.5 m it keeps from the buoy, then returns to the path.
  • Switch to Collect litter. The same few lines run with another cost. Set the second term to zero: the Otter still explores, but no longer keeps what it has seen in view. Raise the drift and see how much it loses to the west.
  • In Collect litter, let it plan its speed too, and watch the speed chart: the plans' speeds wiggle and get their votes just like the turn rates. In our runs it collected a little more, 7.3 of 10 pieces in 200 s on average against 6.5 with the turn rate alone.

How it works

The plans. The current plan is a turn rate rk for each of N steps of Δt. MPPI draws M random wiggles, smooth over a few steps, and adds them to it, each turn rate kept within ±rmax:

ri,k = rk + εi,k,   εi,k = α εi,k−1 + √(1−α²) σ ξ,   ξ ~ 𝒩(0, 1)

The model (kinematic, at a constant speed, forward Euler) predicts where each plan goes:

xnk+1 = xnk + Δt U cos ψk,   ynk+1 = ynk + Δt U sin ψk,   ψk+1 = ψk + Δt ri,k

The votes. Each plan gets a cost Ji and a weight, and the new plan is their weighted average. Weighting random samples like this is called importance sampling. The Otter carries out the first step, the plan moves one step on, and it all starts again from where the Otter really is:

wi ∝ exp(−(Ji − min J) / T),   rk = Σi wi ri,k

Following a path. Ji = Σ ye,k2 + λ Σ ri,k2, with ye the distance from the path ahead of the Otter. The path's corners are the symmetric polynomial transition curves of the LOS Guidance app. A plan into water shallower than 1.5 m, within 3.5 m of a buoy or within dsafe of the other Otter, predicted straight on, gets so high a cost that its vote is nothing: it is dropped. The defaults: M = 1000, Δt = 0.5 s, N = 60 (30 s ahead), σ = 3 deg/s, α = 0.8, T = 40, λ = 0.05, rmax = 10 deg/s. The video looks 15 s ahead and turns at most 8 deg/s.

Collecting litter. The same MPPI with another cost, in 1 s steps over 30 s: the water a plan would bring into the camera's view, the more so if it has not been looked at lately; the litter it remembers, at the place its drift should have taken it, kept in view; a soft wall round the area; and hard turns. Within 15 m of a piece in view, a simple pursuit takes over and steers it into the net between the hulls, unless that would take it into shallow water. The Otter knows the drift only roughly and sees a piece only inside the camera's view, up to 30 m away. The defaults are the video's: M = 150, σ = 4 deg/s, α = 0.8, T = 3.

Speed as a second input (not in the video). Each plan then also has a speed Ui,k for every step, the current plan's plus a wiggle of its own, smooth in the same way with size σU, and kept between 0.3 m/s and Umax. The model moves each step at that plan's speed, and a fifth term, AU Σ (Ui,k − U)2, keeps it near the speed U. The new speeds are the same weighted average as the turn rates. The Otter's speed follows with a lag of 1.5 s, which the model leaves out, and the pursuit holds U. This is a simplified version of the planner in MPPI-based Informative Trajectory Planning for Search and Capture of Drifting Targets with ASVs, which was tested in field trials with a USV. Its code is on GitHub: mppi-search-and-capture.

The Otter, a 2 m USV made by Maritime Robotics, is simulated here as the same kinematic model, every 20 ms, except that its turn rate follows the commanded one with a first-order lag τ, which the model leaves out. The depths are those of the video's digital twin of the strait, at low resolution; don't navigate by them.
